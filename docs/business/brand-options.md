# Brand options for Private Skills

Status: provisional naming brief, prepared 2026-09-15. No name is selected.

The product brief describes a private, policy-controlled entry point for an
engineering team's own and upstream agent capabilities: a registry, a
skills.sh-style pull-through proxy, scanned immutable releases, curated packs,
Eve-assisted authoring/review, and a cross-platform CLI. The current binary is
`pskills`; the name below should be able to grow beyond a single skill format,
provider, or deployment shape. See [`README.md`](../../README.md) and
[`docs/product.md`](../product.md).

## Provisional recommendation

The current order is:

1. **Packrail** — clearest immediate story for governed packs and a controlled
   path from source to install.
2. **Brintra** — strongest broad umbrella if the business expands beyond a
   registry; it is coined and therefore needs spelling work.
3. **Gusset** — strongest engineering metaphor for joining and reinforcing
   sources, scans, packs, and provider identities; it has the highest
   pronunciation and “what does that mean?” risk.

The other three options are useful fallbacks, with explicit reasons to keep
them below the top three. These are naming candidates, not clearance results.

## Shortlist

### 1. Packrail

**Pronunciation:** PACK-rayl

**Idea:** A rail is a controlled route; a pack is a versioned collection of
capabilities. Together, Packrail says that a team can move approved agent
capabilities from source to install along a visible, policy-bound path.

**Positioning:** The trusted release rail for an engineering team's agent
capabilities.

**Tagline:** “A trusted rail for agent capabilities.”

**Why it fits:** It maps directly to packs, pull-through feeds, immutable
versions, provenance, scanning, and the CLI workflow. It is understandable in
a developer conversation without putting “agent” or “skill” in the company
name. It can still cover individual skills, packs, and future capability types.

**Risks:** “Pack” can make the product sound limited to collections, while
“rail” has logistics and payments associations. The quick screen found adjacent
lexical noise in [Packr](https://www.packr.io/), a 3PL customer-support
platform, and [Packsmith](https://packsmith.ai/), an AI fulfillment and
distribution platform. No obvious exact Packrail product surfaced in the
bounded search.

**Brand architecture:** Packrail Registry, Packrail Feeds, Packrail Packs,
Packrail Scan, Packrail Studio, and Packrail Connect. Keep `pskills` as the
implementation CLI until the product name is chosen.

**Domain directions:** `packrail.dev`, `packrail.systems`,
`usepackrail.com`, `docs.packrail.dev` (availability unverified).

### 2. Brintra

**Pronunciation:** BRIN-truh

**Idea:** A coined blend of “bridge” and “intra”: a controlled bridge that
brings approved external capabilities inside an organization.

**Positioning:** The private control plane that brings trusted agent
capabilities into the tools and boundaries an engineering team already owns.

**Tagline:** “Bring trusted capabilities inside.”

**Why it fits:** Brintra is broad enough for multi-tenant hosting,
multi-provider login, source discovery, scanner policy, packs, and authoring.
It does not imply that the company is only a package registry or only a
security gateway. The coined form also gives the business room to define its
own meaning.

**Risks:** The meaning is not self-evident, and people may spell or pronounce
it as “Brinter.” An exact-name public product/company search did not surface an
obvious direct software conflict in this bounded screen; that is a discovery
result, not legal clearance.

**Brand architecture:** Brintra Catalog, Brintra Sources, Brintra Policy,
Brintra Packs, Brintra Studio, and Brintra Connect. The product descriptor can
do the explanatory work: “Brintra, the private registry for governed agent
capabilities.”

**Domain directions:** `brintra.dev`, `brintra.systems`, `getbrintra.com`,
`docs.brintra.dev` (availability unverified).

### 3. Gusset

**Pronunciation:** GUS-it

**Idea:** In structural engineering, a gusset is a plate that joins and
reinforces members at a connection. That is a useful metaphor for joining
sources, scanner evidence, policies, packs, and provider identities into one
reliable operating surface.

**Positioning:** The engineering-grade connection layer for the capabilities
your agents are allowed to use.

**Tagline:** “Reinforce every capability your agents use.”

**Why it fits:** The name speaks to engineering teams and avoids a narrow
marketplace or assistant identity. It supports a story about interoperability,
structural trust, and clear seams between providers and team policy.

**Risks:** It is an uncommon word outside engineering and apparel. Buyers may
need the pronunciation and metaphor explained. The quick screen found no
obvious exact Gusset software product; an unrelated [Gus AI back-office
product](https://www.uscv.ai/) creates minor lexical noise.

**Brand architecture:** Gusset Catalog, Gusset Links, Gusset Scan, Gusset
Packs, Gusset Studio, and Gusset Connect. “Gusset for agent capabilities” is a
useful early descriptor while awareness is built.

**Domain directions:** `gusset.dev`, `gusset.systems`, `usegusset.com`,
`docs.gusset.dev` (availability unverified).

### 4. Inletmark

**Pronunciation:** IN-let-mark

**Idea:** A controlled inlet is where something enters a system; a mark records
that it entered with an identity or proof. The compound connects ingress,
provenance, and policy without using “secure” or “agent” as a generic suffix.

**Positioning:** The verified ingress for external agent capabilities entering
an engineering environment.

**Tagline:** “Every capability enters with proof.”

**Why it fits:** It makes the source-to-registry boundary and provenance story
easy to explain. It works for individual skills, packs, feeds, and future
provider connectors.

**Risks:** It is literal and a little infrastructure-heavy; “inlet” can evoke
plumbing. An exact-name public product/company search did not surface an
obvious direct software conflict in this bounded screen.

**Brand architecture:** Inletmark Registry, Inletmark Intake, Inletmark Proof,
Inletmark Packs, Inletmark Studio, and Inletmark Connect.

**Domain directions:** `inletmark.dev`, `inletmark.systems`,
`useinletmark.com`, `docs.inletmark.dev` (availability unverified).

### 5. Sourceward

**Pronunciation:** SOURCE-ward

**Idea:** A direct compound for putting every upstream source under team policy
and preserving where a capability came from.

**Positioning:** Source-aware governance for the agent capability supply chain.

**Tagline:** “Bring every source under policy.”

**Why it fits:** It explains the source catalog, pull-through, provenance,
scanner evidence, and access-control story with very little copy. It is the
most immediately descriptive option after Packrail.

**Risks:** The `-ward` pattern is already busy in adjacent AI governance and
security products: [Stateward](https://www.stateward.com/) describes an
autonomous security agent for a software estate, and [Trustward](https://trustward.ai/)
describes governance for apps built with AI. No exact Sourceward product
surfaced in the bounded search, but this suffix crowding is why it is a
fallback rather than a recommendation.

**Brand architecture:** Sourceward Registry, Sourceward Sources, Sourceward
Policy, Sourceward Packs, Sourceward Studio, and Sourceward Connect.

**Domain directions:** `sourceward.dev`, `sourceward.systems`,
`usesourceward.com`, `docs.sourceward.dev` (availability unverified).

### 6. Veridock

**Pronunciation:** VERR-ih-dok

**Idea:** A coined blend of “verifiable” and “dock”: a controlled landing place
where a capability is checked, identified, and made available to its intended
team.

**Positioning:** The verified landing zone for private agent capabilities.

**Tagline:** “Land capabilities with proof.”

**Why it fits:** It gives the product a memorable place metaphor while leaving
room for registry, packs, scanner evidence, and provider routing. “Dock” also
works naturally with intake and release language.

**Risks:** “Dock” may make some developers think first of Docker or a hosting
product, and “veri-” is a common naming prefix. An exact-name public
product/company search did not surface an obvious direct software conflict in
this bounded screen.

**Brand architecture:** Veridock Registry, Veridock Intake, Veridock Proof,
Veridock Packs, Veridock Studio, and Veridock Connect.

**Domain directions:** `veridock.dev`, `veridock.systems`, `useveridock.com`,
`docs.veridock.dev` (availability unverified).

## Names screened out for obvious product overlap

These names were attractive on meaning but were removed from the shortlist
after a quick public-source check because a directly adjacent product already
uses the name:

- **Parapet** — [runtime authorization for AI agents](https://parapet.run/)
  and a separate [self-hosted LLM policy gateway](https://parapetai.com/).
- **Spandrel** — a [graph, REST, MCP, and skills publishing
  platform](https://spandrel.org/).
- **Cairn** — multiple developer/agent products, including
  [AI-native owned applications](https://www.getcairns.dev/) and a
  [multi-provider agent runtime](https://cairn.computer/docs/backends).
- **Cleat** — an [auditable AI orchestration language](https://cleat-lang.org/)
  and an [AI coding-agent tool](https://cleat.sh/docs/).
- **Ferrule** — a [multi-tenant MCP gateway](https://docs.ferrule.io/), close
  to the proposed registry and access-control role.
- **Sluice** — a [registry/fetcher/feed gateway](https://github.com/zlef-fr/sluice),
  close to pull-through behavior.
- **Rafter** — a [security CLI for AI coding agents](https://docs.rafter.so/),
  including extension auditing and policy enforcement.
- **Stanchion** — [runtime governance for AI coding agents](https://stanchionai.com/).
- **Armature** — [agent discoverability/search](https://armature.tech/) and
  [continuous testing for MCP and CLI agents](https://www.tryarmature.com/).
- **Lamina** — a [transactional product graph for AI coding agents](https://lamina.dev/)
  with skills and a CLI.
- **Cordon** — [guardrails for coding agents](https://cordon.sh/).
- **Gateward** — an [LLM control plane and model gateway](https://gateward.ai/).
- **Provenrail** — [agent guardrails and verifiable audit trails](https://provenrail.com/).

The screen is intentionally bounded. It is enough to remove obvious collisions
from this naming pass, not enough to establish trademark rights, company-name
rights, or domain ownership.

## Recommendation for the next decision

Use **Packrail** for the first naming test if the business wants the product
story to be understood immediately by engineering buyers. Use **Brintra** if
the priority is a broader, more ownable umbrella that can absorb future
provider and authoring products. Keep **Gusset** as the memorable engineering
alternative if a more technical voice is desirable.

Before committing, test the top three with five engineering buyers using only
the name, pronunciation, and one-line descriptor; then perform a dedicated
trademark/domain review for the selected candidate. Until that decision, keep
the current `pskills` CLI, configuration keys, package identifiers, and UI copy
replaceable.
